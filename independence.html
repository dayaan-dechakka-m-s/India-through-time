Independence Movement

Nationalism • Mass Politics • Protest • 1947

🇮🇳 The Road to Independence

Indian independence was the result of a long and complex political movement involving many organizations, leaders, social groups and regional movements.

Different communities participated in different ways, and nationalist politics contained multiple viewpoints.

Congress Founded 1885
Non-Cooperation 1920–22
Civil Disobedience 1930s
Independence 15 August 1947

👥 Who Participated?

Students, workers, farmers, women, business communities, lawyers, writers, political activists and local organizers participated in different phases.

Participation was not identical across India. Regional movements and local political priorities were important.

✊ Major Movements

🔥 Swadeshi Movement

The movement encouraged indigenous production and opposition to the partition of Bengal.

🚫 Non-Cooperation

The movement encouraged withdrawal from selected colonial institutions and forms of cooperation.

🧂 Civil Disobedience

The Salt March became a major symbol of civil disobedience.

📣 Quit India

The 1942 movement demanded an end to British rule.

🗺️ Political Geography

British India consisted of directly administered provinces and princely states. Nationalist politics developed within this complicated political structure.

Regional Movements

04

Partition

The end of colonial rule was accompanied by Partition.

📜 1947

India became independent on 15 August 1947. Independence was accompanied by the Partition of British India and the creation of India and Pakistan.

Partition caused enormous migration, displacement, violence and long-term social consequences.

🧠 Exam Revision

  • The independence movement developed over many decades.
  • The Indian National Congress was one major nationalist organization.
  • Swadeshi, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience and Quit India were important movements.
  • Many social groups participated in different ways.
  • India became independent on 15 August 1947.
  • Partition was a major and traumatic part of the transition.
