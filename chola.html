✦ SOUTH INDIAN HISTORY ✦

Chola Dynasty

Kaveri Delta • Temples • Irrigation • Maritime Networks

🛕 Chola Power

The imperial Cholas became a major power in South India from around the ninth century CE.

Their political centre was closely associated with the fertile Kaveri delta and Tamil region.

Period c. 9th–13th centuries
Core Kaveri delta
Famous Rulers Rajaraja I and Rajendra I
Famous Monument Brihadisvara Temple

👑 Important Rulers

Vijayalaya is associated with the rise of imperial Chola power.

Rajaraja I expanded Chola territory and sponsored major temple construction.

Rajendra I continued expansion and developed Chola influence across maritime networks.

🗺️ Territory

01

Tamil Region

The Kaveri delta was the central agricultural and political base.

02

Deccan

Chola rulers competed with and sometimes controlled areas outside the Tamil heartland.

03

Sri Lanka

Parts of Sri Lanka came under Chola control at different times.

04

Bay of Bengal

Chola political and commercial activity was connected to maritime networks.

🌾 Agriculture & Irrigation

🌾 Kaveri Delta

The fertile delta supported intensive agriculture and dense settlements.

💧 Irrigation

Tanks, channels and other water-management systems supported cultivation.

🏘️ Villages

Rural communities formed an important part of the economic foundation of Chola power.

🛕 Temples

Temples could function as religious, economic and institutional centres.

🚢 Maritime Trade

The Chola period was connected to Bay of Bengal and Indian Ocean trading networks. Merchant groups, ports and political relationships connected South India with Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia.

🎨 Art & Architecture

🛕 Temples

Large stone temples became important expressions of Chola kingship and devotion.

🪨 Sculpture

Chola bronze sculpture is especially famous for its technical and artistic sophistication.

📜 Inscriptions

Temple inscriptions record donations, administration, land and social institutions.

🧠 Exam Revision

  • Chola power was rooted in the Kaveri delta.
  • Rajaraja I and Rajendra I were major rulers.
  • Irrigation supported agricultural production.
  • Temples were important religious and institutional centres.
  • Chola political power was connected to maritime networks.
